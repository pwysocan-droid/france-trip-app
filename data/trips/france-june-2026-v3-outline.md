# France 2026 — Nice to Cap Ferret to Paris

**June 14 to July 1, 2026.** Three of us, 17 nights, three chapters. Arrive Nice by air on the evening of June 14, depart Paris CDG on July 1 by DL290 at 4pm.

## Why this shape

The trip has three movements. The south (June 14–21, 7 nights) traces a line through how Mediterranean people live in considered relationship to the sea, the rocks, and the village — from Le Corbusier's seaside cabanon to two nights on a car-free island to Marseille's vertical city to three nights at a private estate in the Luberon. Cap Ferret (June 21–27, 6 nights) is the deceleration — friends' house, beach, oysters, no agenda. Paris (June 27 to July 1, 4 nights) is the metropolitan close — Les Puces, museums, neighborhoods, last meals.

The trip is bookended by long-haul flights and stitched together by two TGVs: Narbonne → Bordeaux on June 21, Bordeaux → Paris on June 27.

This document covers the southern leg in detail. Cap Ferret and Paris are sketched at the end and live in chat for now; both will get full treatments in their own time.

The argument of this version of the trip is *Mediterranean living* rather than *modernism on tour*. Where the earlier drafts collected modernist institutions (Maeght, Vasarely, Cézanne's atelier), this version commits to a tighter editorial line: the places that show how to live considered with the Mediterranean — Eileen Gray's E-1027 and Le Corbusier's Cabanon as foundational examples; Porquerolles as the island chapter (with Fondation Carmignac as the contemporary art presence); Cité Radieuse as the post-war scaling-up; Tuba as the cabanon ethos returned to as a meal; Loïntes Bastides and Lourmarin as the village that predates and outlasts all of it.

For Nève, the cinephile thread continues: Hitchcock at Cours Saleya if we make it to the Nice market; Berri's *Jean de Florette* and *Manon des Sources* mapped onto Vaugines and Mirabeau (a five-minute drive from Lourmarin); optionally Varda in Sète if we add her on a future trip.

## Day 0 — Saturday June 13: Travel

Depart LAX for Paris CDG. Standard transatlantic. Sleep on the plane.

## Day 1 — Sunday June 14: Arrival, Villefranche-sur-Mer

The trip's landing. A noon arrival means the day has shape — afternoon to settle, swim, walk the old town; dinner on the harbor at the four-generations table where Cocteau ate.

**Land Nice noon (LAX overnight).** Pick up rental car at NCE airport. Drive `Nice → Villefranche-sur-Mer: ~25 min` via the Basse Corniche (M6098) along the coast.

**Sleep:** **Hôtel Le Versailles** ◆, Villefranche-sur-Mer. Family-run hotel on the heights above the rade, with an outdoor pool, panoramic terrace overlooking the Bay of Villefranche-sur-Mer and the Cap-Ferrat peninsula across the water. Triple sea-view room booked. The hotel sits five minutes uphill from the old town and the harbor; everything is walkable from there.

**Afternoon:** check in, pool, settle. Late afternoon walk down through Villefranche's old town — the medieval *Rue Obscure* (a 130-meter covered passage from the 14th century, hidden beneath the harbor houses), the small fishing port, the colored facades of the quay. The **Chapelle Saint-Pierre des Pêcheurs** sits directly on the harbor — a small 14th-century chapel decorated by Jean Cocteau in 1957 with frescoes inspired by the lives of the local fishermen and the life of Saint Peter. Open during the day, free to enter, a five-minute pause that anchors the trip's first cinephile thread for Nève: she'll see Cocteau's hand here, then again in his films.

**Evening:** dinner at **La Mère Germaine** ◆, on the quay since 1938 — the Brau-Blouin family in the kitchen and on the floor for four generations. The terrasse extends onto the quay itself, a few centimeters from the water. 7pm reservation, terrace seaside seating. Bouillabaisse is the institutional order if you want the full ritual; the daily-catch grilled fish is the lighter Day 1 option. Walk back up to Le Versailles after dinner — about 8 minutes uphill, the harbor lights below as you climb.

**For Nève:** Cocteau wrote *Orphée* (1950) in this village while staying at the Welcome Hôtel two doors down from where you'll have dinner. Mère Germaine and Cocteau were close friends during the 1957 chapel-decoration period. The Cocteau thread that begins tonight extends through the next morning's drive (the *To Catch a Thief* corniche) and into Paris later in the trip.

## Day 2 — Monday June 15: Cap Moderne, drive west, Porquerolles

The day pivots from the eastern Riviera to the western Mediterranean. The morning drive is itself the trip's first cinephile sequence: the Moyenne Corniche east through the documented filming locations of Hitchcock's *To Catch a Thief* (1955).

**Morning — Moyenne Corniche to Cap Moderne.** Breakfast on Le Versailles' panoramic terrace, leave by 8:30am. Drive `Villefranche → Roquebrune-Cap-Martin: ~25 min` via the Moyenne Corniche (M6007). The route passes **La Turbie** (the Trophée d'Auguste — Augustus's victory monument over the Ligurians, visible above the road — and the village through which Robie's car races during the chase scene) and the **Èze viaduct** (the towering arched bridge into Èze village, the bridge in the chase). Both are documented filming locations. *Saint-Jeannet, where Robie's villa is in the film, sits just inland — visible from the autoroute but a real detour for another trip.*

**Cap Moderne tour, 10am ◆.** [BOOKED] The two-hour French-language guided tour: E-1027 (Eileen Gray, 1929), Le Corbusier's Cabanon (1952), the Étoile de Mer beach restaurant. The high point of the modernism arc and the foundational example of the Mediterranean-living thread the trip follows.

**Lunch — Étoile de Mer**, the beach restaurant directly below E-1027, the same spot Le Corbusier ate with the Rebutato family. Long lunch on the rocks looking back across the bay.

**Drive `Roquebrune → Tour Fondue (Hyères): ~2 hr`** via the A8/A50 westbound. Realistic departure ~3pm after lunch, arrive Tour Fondue ~5pm.

**Ferry to Porquerolles** with TLV-TVM. 15-minute crossing. Aim for a 5:30 or 6pm departure so you arrive on the island in the late afternoon light.

**Settle in Porquerolles village.** Check in to lodging. Walk the Place d'Armes square. Quiet first-evening dinner — the day-trip ferries finish around 6:30pm and the island's tempo shifts immediately.

**Sleep:** Porquerolles ◇ — **Le Mas du Langoustier** (the institution, on the western tip with vineyards and pines, restaurant L'Olivier on-site, one Michelin star), **Villa Sainte Anne** (on the village square, smaller and more boutique), or **Hôtel Le Porquerollais** (smaller still, family-run).

## Day 3 — Tuesday June 16: Porquerolles full day

The island's full day. The Tuba register at the scale of an island.

**Morning:** breakfast slow, then bike rental in the village (~€15-20/day). Ride out to one of the north-shore beaches — Plage Notre-Dame, Plage de la Courtade, or Plage d'Argent. Swim. The water is clear; the island is mostly national park. About 30-40 min by bike from the village.

**Late morning — Fondation Carmignac.** Private contemporary art collection (Lichtenstein, Warhol, Basquiat, Cattelan, plus rotating shows) in a restored Provençal *mas* with a sunken sculpture garden by Tom Stuart-Smith. Opened 2018. About 30 minutes' walk from the village or 10 minutes by bike. Real museum, not an island curiosity. Reserve a timed ticket online.

**Lunch — La Tonnelle** in the village square. Pierre Gagnaire-trained chef Marc Saudejaud, vine-shaded courtyard, contemporary-Provençal at one notch above bistrot register. Reserve a few days ahead.

**Afternoon:** another beach, or a vineyard visit at Domaine de la Courtade or Domaine Perzinsky (the island's own AOC Côtes de Provence). Or simply read by the water.

**Evening:** dinner at the hotel restaurant or in the village. The island is genuinely empty of day-trippers after 6:30pm — eat slowly.

**Sleep:** Porquerolles, same lodging.

## Day 4 — Wednesday June 17: Marseille day

The day Tuba and Cité Radieuse land. The structural day of the trip — three different scales of "how to live" in coherent order.

**Early morning:** pack the night before. Catch the **8 or 8:30am ferry** from Porquerolles to Tour Fondue (15 min). Light breakfast at the hotel or skipped — coffee in Marseille shortly.

**Drive `Tour Fondue → Marseille (1st arrondissement): ~1h15m`** via the A50. Park near Cours Saint-Louis (Centre-Bourse).

**Mid-morning — Maison Empereur**, rue des Récolettes. The eight-generation hardware shop selling everything for living a French domestic life — knives, brushes, soap, copper pots, linens, baskets. Eight floors of considered French objects. About 1.5-2 hours. Worth thinking ahead about what you actually want from the visit; the place rewards specific intent over wandering.

**Drive `Empereur → Tuba Club at Les Goudes: ~25 min`** along the corniche.

**Lunch — Tuba Club, 1pm ◆** [BOOKED]. Long lunch with swim between courses, 3-4 hours. Bring swimsuits. The Marseille day's anchor and the trip's loved-it return. Plan to leave the table around 4:30pm.

**Drive `Les Goudes → Cité Radieuse: ~25 min`** back through the corniche. Arrive ~5pm.

**Late afternoon — Cité Radieuse.** Boulevard Michelet, 8th arrondissement. The roof terrace closes at 6pm — you have an hour. The post-war "vertical garden city" that scales the Mediterranean-living argument from one cabanon to 1,600 residents. Roof terrace at golden hour facing west, the Modulor playground, the running track, the sea visible through Le Corbusier's framed openings. Walk a level or two of the interior streets if there's time. The MaMo art center on the roof if there's a show worth seeing.

**Drive `Marseille → Cassis: ~30 min`** along the corniche. Stop at Vallon des Auffes for sunset light if there's time.

**Sleep:** Cassis ◇ — **Les Roches Blanches** (the address, perched above the harbor with the Cap Canaille view), **Hôtel de la Plage Mahogany** (beachside, mid-range), or **Hôtel Le Liautaud** (on the harbor, traditional).

**Evening:** quiet dinner in Cassis or skip if Tuba was big enough.

## Day 5 — Thursday June 18: Cassis → Lourmarin

The transition to the slow center.

**Morning:** slow Cassis morning. Coffee on the harbor. Calanque de Port-Miou walk (20 minutes from the village center) if energy and weather allow — easy first calanque without committing to a hike. Pack.

**Drive `Cassis → Lourmarin: ~1.5 hr`** via the A7 north past Aix.

**Mid-afternoon:** arrive **Loïntes Bastides** ◆. 16 Chemin des Lointes Bastides. Seventeenth-century private estate, five acres, fifteen-meter pool, Aiguebrun river running through, vineyards and ancient olive groves, fifteen-minute walk to the village. Settle in. Swim. Three nights.

**Evening:** dinner in Lourmarin village. Something casual — save the Michelin lunch for tomorrow.

**Sleep:** Loïntes Bastides ◆ — Night 1 of 3.

## Day 6 — Friday June 19: Lourmarin slow + cinephile half-day

The trip's slow center begins.

**Morning:** coffee in the village. Walk Lourmarin without crowds. **Camus's grave** in the cemetery above town — quiet, unmarked, a simple stone. If Nève has read a few pages of *L'Étranger* before the trip, the visit becomes literary.

**Mid-morning to early afternoon — the cinephile half-day.** The *Jean de Florette / Manon des Sources* pilgrimage:

- **Vaugines** (`~5 min` from Lourmarin): église Saint-Barthélémy (the wedding, the final sermon), the cemetery (the bench scene). 30-45 minutes.
- **Mirabeau** (`~30 min` from Vaugines): the fountain (the dry one Manon caused), Café de la Fontaine, the school-now-mairie. Coffee at the café. 45 minutes.

Drive `→ Cucuron: ~30 min`.

**Lunch — La Petite Maison de Cucuron.** Place de l'Étang. Eric Sapet, one Michelin star. Reserve 2-3 weeks ahead. 2.5-3 hours.

**Late afternoon — Place de l'Étang.** Long café on the platanes-shaded square next door. The mirror of water, the slow light. The purest Provence-afternoon of the trip.

Drive `Cucuron → Loïntes Bastides: ~15 min`.

**Evening:** dinner in or at the estate. Don't pile big meals.

**Sleep:** Loïntes Bastides ◆ — Night 2 of 3.

## Day 7 — Saturday June 20: pure Loïntes day

The slowest day of the trip, deliberately.

Slow morning at the estate. Pool, river, garden. Lourmarin's Saturday market in the village (the smaller one — Friday's is the big one, but you arrive after it). Lunch at the estate or a casual village bistrot. Long pool / river afternoon. Possibly drive 30 minutes to one of the perched villages — Bonnieux, Ménerbes, Gordes — for golden hour. Or don't.

This is the day that earns the third night at Loïntes Bastides. The trip has slack here on purpose.

**Evening:** last dinner in the Luberon. Lourmarin village has good options.

**Sleep:** Loïntes Bastides ◆ — Night 3 of 3.

## Day 8 — Sunday June 21: Train to Cap Ferret

The transition west.

**Morning:** slow breakfast at Loïntes Bastides. Pack. Leave by ~9:30am to give buffer.

Drive `Lourmarin → Narbonne: ~3 hr` via the A7 + A9.

Drop the rental car at Narbonne station. Verify drop-off contractually with Avis or Hertz before booking — Narbonne isn't always default, but most chains permit it.

**TGV Narbonne → Bordeaux:** `~3 hr` direct. Book mid-March 2026 when SNCF's 90-day window opens.

**Pick up rental car at Bordeaux Saint-Jean.** Drive `Bordeaux → Cap Ferret: ~1 hr` via A630 + D106. Arrive evening at the friends' house on Plage Américaine.

End of the southern leg. Cap Ferret begins.

## What this draft deliberately doesn't include

**Maeght and Colombe d'Or.** Saint-Paul-de-Vence's two big draws — Sert's 1964 Foundation building and the legendary art-saturated inn — are gone for this trip in favor of two nights on Porquerolles. Both are durable destinations easy to plan future weekends around. The trade is genuinely editorial: away from "tour the modernism museums," toward "live in Mediterranean places."

**La Celle.** The Lucrèce de Barras suite at Hostellerie de l'Abbaye de la Celle was the still-center of v1 and is genuinely missed. Deferred to a future trip — La Celle is the kind of place to go *to*, on its own weekend, rather than fitting into a moving itinerary.

**Sète and the Languedoc coast.** v1 ended at Sète with oysters and Valéry's grave; this version routes north to Cap Ferret instead. The Languedoc chapter is gone for this trip.

**Aix-en-Provence and Vasarely.** Vasarely Foundation and Cézanne's atelier (which reopens July 2026, after this trip) wait for next time.

**Villa Noailles in Hyères.** The missing first chapter of the modernism arc, not on the route. Worth knowing it's only 30 minutes from Tour Fondue and could be added on a future trip with Porquerolles.

**Frank Gehry's Luma in Arles.** Worth seeing but doesn't fit this trip's specific argument.
